    Helpdesk — Tickets, SLAs, Support Workflow¶

    What it does¶

    Helpdesk turns support chaos into a queue: every request becomes an HD Ticket with a type, a priority, an owner, and a clock. The clock is the Service Level Agreement — first response and resolution targets that the team is measured against. Tickets arrive from email, the customer portal or a form; agents work them in a team; and the knowledge base (HD Article) answers the question before it becomes a ticket. Frappe Helpdesk is a separate app (bench get-app helpdesk), renumbered to v16.x in November 2026 — unlimited agents, no per-seat fee.

    Core DocTypes¶

    DocType What it is
    HD Ticket One request: customer, subject, type, priority, status, agent, SLA.
    HD Agent A support agent: who can be assigned tickets.
    HD Team A group of agents (e.g. "ERP Support", "Billing").
    HD Service Level Agreement Response and resolution targets per priority and customer.
    HD Service Level Priority One priority's targets inside an SLA (e.g. Urgent: 1 hr response).
    HD Ticket Type The kind of request: Bug, Question, Feature Request.
    HD Ticket Priority Urgent, High, Medium, Low.
    HD Ticket Status Open, Replied, Resolved, Closed.
    HD Customer A customer in Helpdesk, linked to their ERPNext Customer.
    HD Article One knowledge-base article, in a category.
    HD Article Category A section of the knowledge base (Getting Started, Billing).
    HD Saved Reply A canned response for a common answer.
    HD Ticket Template A form template for raising a ticket (fields per type).

    End-to-end flow¶

    Raise:    email / portal / form → HD Ticket (auto-created, type + priority set)
Assign:   ticket → HD Team → HD Agent (round-robin or manual)
Work:     agent replies, ticket moves Open → Replied → Resolved
Clock:    SLA measures first response and resolution against targets
Close:    customer confirms → Closed; feedback requested
Learn:    repeated questions → HD Article → fewer tickets next month
Check:   ticket ageing, SLA breach, first-response time reports
    • A ticket has one owner at a time. An unassigned ticket is a ticket nobody is working on — the queue view makes that visible.
    • The SLA clock starts when the ticket is created and pauses when the agent waits for the customer. Breaches are the report the support lead checks every morning.
    • HD Article is the cheapest support there is: every question answered once in the knowledge base is a ticket that never arrives.
    • A Saved Reply keeps answers consistent — the same question gets the same correct answer, whoever is on shift.

    Worked example — NexTash support, one week¶

    A client's user cannot post a Purchase Receipt:

    1. She emails support. An HD Ticket is auto-created: type Question, priority High, customer linked to their ERPNext Customer.
    2. The ticket lands in the "ERP Support" team queue and is assigned to an agent. The SLA says first response within 4 hours.
    3. The agent finds the cause — a missing permission on the Purchase Receipt DocType — and replies with the fix. Status → Replied.
    4. The client confirms it works. Status → Resolved → Closed.
    5. The same question arrives from a second client. The agent writes an HD Article ("Why you cannot submit a Purchase Receipt") and a Saved Reply pointing to it. The third time, the answer takes 30 seconds.

    A real use case¶

    A software company ran support from a shared inbox. The same question was answered five different ways, urgent requests sat behind routine ones, and nobody knew how many tickets were open. After Helpdesk, the queue showed the real workload, the SLA report showed first-response time dropping from a day to two hours, and the knowledge base cut repeat tickets by a third.

    How we can use it: run NexTash's own client support on Helpdesk — it is the demo and the tool in one. Then sell it to any client whose support runs on a shared inbox or WhatsApp: the SLA report is the pitch ("your customers will know you answer in hours, not days"), and the knowledge base is the phase-2 sale.

    Common mistakes¶

    • No SLA. Without targets, "urgent" is an opinion and response time is whatever happened. Set the SLA on day one.
    • Tickets with no owner. The queue must show unassigned tickets prominently — or they rot.
    • Resolving without confirming. A ticket closed before the customer confirms is a ticket that reopens angrier.
    • No knowledge base. Answering the same question ten times is ten tickets that an article would have prevented.
    • Support in a shared inbox. Email has no queue, no owner, no clock. That is exactly what Helpdesk replaces.
